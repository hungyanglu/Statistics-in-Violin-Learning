# Future Work

Several limitations were identified during the course of this study. Although this research emphasizes the use of objective data to evaluate the effectiveness of instructional strategies, certain subjective elements remain. In both the data collection procedures and operational definitions, some judgments, such as determining the threshold at which a behavior should be classified as problematic, still rely on human interpretation. Since no objective measurement tools were available for these aspects, the recording process inevitably contained a degree of subjectivity.

Furthermore, the reliance on textbook pages as a progress indicator represents another major limitation. Although the *Shinozaki Violin Method* is one of the most widely used violin textbooks in Taiwan, it remains difficult to evaluate or compare students who follow different instructional materials. For example, students may use supplementary materials, such as Wohlfahrt or Kayser studies, to develop their technical skills, making it challenging to estimate their underlying learning levels based solely on textbook progression. Developing a standardized method to quantify the difficulty levels of different textbooks and transform their progress indicators into a common scale remains an important challenge for future research. 

Despite these limitations, this study greatly enhanced my understanding of pedagogical phenomena and student learning behaviors. It allowed me to better anticipate students' learning responses and more efficiently evaluate the effectiveness of instructional strategies. Although some teaching decisions were still unsuccessful, the analytical framework substantially reduced the time spent relying solely on intuition and provided a more systematic approach to instructional decision-making.
